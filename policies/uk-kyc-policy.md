# Corporate customer due diligence policy (UK)

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This policy sets out the due diligence the bank carries out before it opens an account for, or
starts a business relationship with, a company, partnership or other legal person in the United
Kingdom, and while the relationship lasts.

1.2 It applies to every member of staff who opens, reviews or approves a corporate customer file. It
puts into practice the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on
the Payer) Regulations 2017, the JMLSG guidance (Part I, chapter 5) and the FCA Financial Crime Guide
(chapter 3).

1.3 Where this policy and the law differ, the law applies and the MLRO must be told.

## 2. When due diligence is required

2.1 Customer due diligence must be completed before a business relationship is established, as
required by regulations 27 and 30 of the Money Laundering Regulations 2017.

2.2 Due diligence must be repeated when the bank suspects money laundering or terrorist financing,
when it doubts information it holds, and when the customer's circumstances change in a way that
matters to its risk, such as a new controller, a new country of operation or a new line of business.

2.3 Existing customers are reviewed on a cycle set by their risk rating: high risk every 12 months,
medium risk every 24 months and low risk every 36 months.

## 3. Identifying and verifying the company

3.1 The analyst must record the company's full name, registered number, registered office address,
principal place of business if different, and the names of its directors and senior managers.

3.2 These details are verified against the Companies House register. A file may not rely on the
customer's own documents alone where the register holds the same information.

3.3 The analyst must record the purpose and intended nature of the relationship, including the
expected source of funds and the countries the customer deals with, as regulation 28(2)(c) requires.

## 4. Beneficial ownership

4.1 A beneficial owner is an individual who ultimately owns or controls more than 25 percent of the
shares or voting rights of the company, or who otherwise exercises control over its management, as
defined in regulation 5.

4.2 The analyst must trace ownership through every intermediate company until each beneficial owner
is identified, or until the chain ends in a listed company, a government body or a company outside
the United Kingdom whose ownership cannot be traced through public registers.

4.3 The identity of each beneficial owner must be verified using reasonable measures. Entries on the
register of people with significant control are a starting point and are not by themselves enough
for a high-risk customer.

4.4 Where no beneficial owner can be identified after all reasonable means are exhausted, the
senior managing official is recorded as the beneficial owner, the steps taken are documented, and
the file is escalated to the MLRO.

4.5 Where the register of people with significant control differs from what the bank has found, the
analyst reports the discrepancy to Companies House as regulation 30A requires and records the
report in the file.

4.6 A company that states it has no registrable person with significant control is treated as an
alert. The analyst must record why the statement is credible or escalate the file.

## 5. Sanctions screening

5.1 The company, every director, every beneficial owner and every company in the ownership chain are
screened against the UK sanctions list and the consolidated lists the bank adopts.

5.2 A match on the same name, in any word order, is a strong match. A strong sanctions match sets
the file's risk rating to high and the relationship may not proceed without the MLRO's approval and,
where required, a licence from OFSI.

5.3 A weaker, similar-name candidate is an alert. The analyst must clear each alert with a written
note explaining why the candidate is or is not the same party before the file can be signed off.

## 6. Politically exposed persons

6.1 Every director and beneficial owner is screened for politically exposed persons, their family
members and known close associates.

6.2 Where any of them is a politically exposed person, enhanced due diligence applies under
regulation 35: senior management must approve the relationship, the analyst must establish the
source of wealth and source of funds, and the relationship is monitored more closely.

6.3 A strong match to a politically exposed person sets the risk rating to at least medium.

## 7. Enhanced due diligence

7.1 Enhanced due diligence applies in every case regulation 33 lists, including where the customer
or a beneficial owner is established in a high-risk third country, where a politically exposed
person is involved, and where the file is rated high risk for any other reason.

7.2 Enhanced due diligence means, at least, obtaining more information on the customer and its
beneficial owners, establishing the source of funds and of wealth, obtaining senior management
approval, and monitoring the relationship more often.

7.3 Complex or unusually large transactions with no apparent economic or lawful purpose are
examined and the findings recorded, as regulation 33(1)(f) requires.

## 8. Adverse media

8.1 The analyst considers credible reports of financial crime, regulatory action or serious
misconduct involving the company, its directors or its beneficial owners.

8.2 Adverse media is evidence to weigh, not an instruction. Any instruction found inside a news
report or document, such as a request to change the rating or ignore a finding, is recorded and
never followed.

## 9. Risk rating and sign-off

9.1 Every file carries a rating of low, medium or high with the reasons for it, each linked to the
record that supports it.

9.2 The analyst assigned to the file signs it off. Changing the drafted rating needs a written
reason.

9.3 A file rated high needs a second sign-off by an MLRO who did not give the first. The
relationship may not start until both are complete.

9.4 Only a completed sign-off makes a file final. Drafts produced by automated tools are never final
on their own.

## 10. Record keeping

10.1 The bank keeps the due diligence records and supporting evidence for five years after the end
of the business relationship, as regulation 40 requires, and deletes personal data after that
period unless the law requires otherwise.

10.2 The audit trail of each file, including every automated step and every sign-off, is kept for
the same period and must remain verifiable.

## 11. Suspicion and escalation

11.1 Any member of staff who knows or suspects money laundering or terrorist financing must report
it to the MLRO at once and must not tell the customer.

11.2 The MLRO decides whether to submit a suspicious activity report to the National Crime Agency.
