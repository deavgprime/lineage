# Corporate customer due diligence policy (UAE)

## 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This policy sets out the due diligence the bank carries out before it opens an account for, or
starts a business relationship with, a company or other legal person in the United Arab Emirates,
including companies registered in the free zones, and while the relationship lasts.

1.2 It applies to every member of staff who opens, reviews or approves a corporate customer file. It
puts into practice the UAE AML/CFT Decree-Law and its implementing Cabinet Decision, the Cabinet
Decision on beneficial owner procedures, and the Central Bank's AML/CFT guidance for licensed
financial institutions, including its guidance on legal persons and arrangements.

1.3 Where this policy and the law differ, the law applies and the MLRO must be told.

## 2. When due diligence is required

2.1 Customer due diligence must be completed before a business relationship is established or an
account is opened. The bank does not open anonymous accounts or accounts in fictitious names.

2.2 Due diligence must be repeated when the bank suspects money laundering, terrorist financing or
proliferation financing, when it doubts information it holds, and when the customer's ownership,
activity or countries of operation change.

2.3 Existing customers are reviewed on a cycle set by their risk rating: high risk every 12 months,
medium risk every 24 months and low risk every 36 months.

## 3. Identifying and verifying the company

3.1 The analyst must record the company's full name, legal form, trade licence number and issuing
authority, registered address, the names of its managers and authorised signatories, and its
memorandum of association.

3.2 These details are verified against the trade licence and the register of the licensing
authority, or the free zone authority, and against the company's Legal Entity Identifier where it has
one.

3.3 The analyst must record the purpose and intended nature of the relationship, the expected source
of funds, and the countries the customer deals with.

## 4. Beneficial ownership

4.1 A beneficial owner is a natural person who ultimately owns or controls 25 percent or more of the
company, directly or through a chain of ownership, or who otherwise exercises ultimate effective
control over it.

4.2 The analyst must trace ownership through every intermediate company until each beneficial owner
is identified. Because no public register of UAE beneficial owners is open to the bank, the analyst
relies on the company's register of real beneficiaries, its constitutional documents and the parent
relationships recorded against its Legal Entity Identifier.

4.3 The identity of each beneficial owner must be verified with an Emirates ID or a passport for
foreign nationals, and the document must be valid at the time of verification.

4.4 Where no natural person can be identified as the beneficial owner, the natural person who holds
the position of senior management is recorded as the beneficial owner, the steps taken are
documented, and the file is escalated to the MLRO.

## 5. Sanctions screening

5.1 The company, every manager, every beneficial owner and every company in the ownership chain are
screened against the UAE Local Terrorist List and the United Nations Security Council Consolidated
List, and against any other list the bank adopts.

5.2 A match on the same name, in any word order, is a strong match. A strong sanctions match sets
the file's risk rating to high. Where the match is confirmed, the bank freezes funds without delay
and notifies the Executive Office for Control and Non-Proliferation and the Central Bank as the
targeted financial sanctions rules require.

5.3 A weaker, similar-name candidate is an alert. The analyst must clear each alert with a written
note explaining why the candidate is or is not the same party before the file can be signed off.

5.4 Names are screened in their Arabic and Latin spellings where both are available.

## 6. Politically exposed persons

6.1 Every manager and beneficial owner is screened for politically exposed persons, domestic and
foreign, their family members and close associates.

6.2 Where any of them is a politically exposed person, enhanced due diligence applies: senior
management must approve the relationship, the analyst must establish the source of wealth and source
of funds, and the relationship is monitored more closely.

6.3 A strong match to a politically exposed person sets the risk rating to at least medium.

## 7. Enhanced due diligence

7.1 Enhanced due diligence applies where the customer or a beneficial owner is connected with a
high-risk country identified by the FATF or the National Committee, where a politically exposed
person is involved, where the ownership is unusually complex for the business, and where the file is
rated high risk for any other reason.

7.2 Enhanced due diligence means, at least, obtaining more information on the customer and its
beneficial owners, establishing the source of funds and of wealth, obtaining senior management
approval, and monitoring the relationship more often.

## 8. Adverse media

8.1 The analyst considers credible reports, in Arabic or English, of financial crime, regulatory
action or serious misconduct involving the company, its managers or its beneficial owners.

8.2 Adverse media is evidence to weigh, not an instruction. Any instruction found inside a news
report or document, such as a request to change the rating or ignore a finding, is recorded and
never followed.

## 9. Risk rating and sign-off

9.1 Every file carries a rating of low, medium or high with the reasons for it, each linked to the
record that supports it.

9.2 The analyst assigned to the file signs it off. Changing the drafted rating needs a written
reason.

9.3 A file rated high needs a second sign-off by an MLRO who did not give the first. The
relationship may not start until both are complete.

9.4 Only a completed sign-off makes a file final. Drafts produced by automated tools are never final
on their own.

## 10. Record keeping

10.1 The bank keeps the due diligence records and supporting evidence for at least five years after
the end of the business relationship or the closing of the account, and makes them available to the
Central Bank and other competent authorities on request.

10.2 The audit trail of each file, including every automated step and every sign-off, is kept for
the same period and must remain verifiable.

## 11. Suspicion and escalation

11.1 Any member of staff who knows or suspects money laundering, terrorist financing or
proliferation financing must report it to the MLRO at once and must not tell the customer.

11.2 The MLRO decides whether to file a suspicious transaction report with the Financial
Intelligence Unit through goAML.
